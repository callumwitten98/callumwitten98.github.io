I'm a postdoctoral researcher at the [Cavendish Laboratory](https://www.phy.cam.ac.uk/), University of Cambridge, working with Roberto Maiolino. I study galaxies in the first billion years of the Universe, combining observations from the James Webb Space Telescope and ESO's Very Large Telescope with cosmological simulations.

My research asks how the first galaxies and black holes formed and grew, and how their surroundings shaped them, from galaxy mergers to the dense protoclusters that will go on to become today's largest galaxy clusters. Together, these early galaxies drove reionisation, the last major transformation of the Universe, and I want to understand how and when that happened.

I'm PI of [JWST programme 4043](https://www.stsci.edu/jwst/science-execution/program-information?id=4043) and of several VLT programmes using KMOS and X-shooter.

Before returning to Cambridge in 2026, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Geneva in Pascal Oesch's [Galaxy Build-up at Cosmic Dawn](https://www.unige.ch/sciences/astro/cosmicdawn/) group. I did my PhD at the Institute of Astronomy in Cambridge (2021–2024), supervised by [Debora Sijacki](https://www.ast.cam.ac.uk/people/debora.sijacki) and [Nicolas Laporte](https://www.nicolas-laporte.com/), with a thesis on primeval galaxies in the era of JWST, and was awarded the 2025 Murdin Prize for the best publication by a PhD student. Before that, I studied for an MPhys in Physics with Astrophysics at the University of Bath.

See my [research](/research), [publications](/publications/) or [CV](/cv/).
